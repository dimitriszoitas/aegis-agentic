# Motion

Motion explains a change of state or position. Keep feedback immediate and transitions short enough that they do not delay the next action.

## Duration and easing

The semantic timing roles are `--semantic-motion-fast`, `--semantic-motion-base`, and `--semantic-motion-slide`; `--semantic-ease-out` supplies the settling curve. They reference primitive durations and curve coordinates directly. Components select them through group tokens such as `--group-trigger-button-motion-fast`. Choose a role that describes the transition rather than imposing one duration on every component.

## Navigation and surfaces

Sidebar width and padding use `--group-navigation-motion-fast`. Labels fade and clip without wrapping, and the toggle remains a stable target. Fundamental uses edge-aligned navigation and workspace panels. Fundamental Floating contains navigation and assistant panels with rounded corners and exterior insets.

Switching presentation preserves font, size, spacing, content, and interaction state. Styling roles can map differently across the four configurations. Corner geometry is not animated. Opening a surface must also maintain its keyboard and focus behavior; animation alone does not provide that behavior.

## Reduced motion

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` in every animation and transition. State changes must remain understandable when movement is removed. A loading or streaming indicator should explain progress without preventing interaction with the rest of the workspace.

Avoid automatic decorative loops. When continuous movement is necessary, use the component's reduced-motion treatment and keep the underlying status available as text. The timing example above moves only after activation and leaves focus on its button.
